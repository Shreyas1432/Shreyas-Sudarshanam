02  Flagship project

Master's thesis · deployed live

Procurement Governance Intelligence

A leakage-audited analytics framework over 12.1 million Italian public procurement contracts, built to survive GDPR Article 22 scrutiny, not just to score well.

The problem

Prior procurement-risk models often re-learn their own label instead of predicting risk, validate network structure against a single unreliable metric, or produce results a regulator can't inspect. GDPR Article 22 requires the last one to be solvable.

What I built

Network analysis. A 161,695-node bipartite buyer-supplier graph in NetworkX. Composite centrality and Louvain community detection, validated against three independent topology-native criteria rather than a single modularity score.

Governance-risk classification. A 20+ check automated leakage audit that screened thirteen candidate features down to one genuinely non-leaky predictor, with the other twelve logged as reasoned nulls. Random Forest, XGBoost, and Logistic Regression trained under a strict temporal split (train 2014–2018, validate 2019, test 2020). XGBoost reached 0.826 ROC–AUC on the 2020 hold-out.

Price anomaly detection. A three-detector unsupervised consensus ensemble (Isolation Forest, Local Outlier Factor, Elliptic Envelope) over category-restricted price residuals, calibrated to a 5.08% audit-prioritisation shortlist.

Design discipline. Interpretable tree and graph methods chosen over deep learning specifically for auditability. Every rejected feature and method logged as a reasoned decision, not a silent drop.

Shipped, not just modelled

Deployed as a public Streamlit dashboard with five operational views: supplier risk, buyer dependency, governance risk mapping, competition analytics, and price variance. It reads only the pipeline's standardised outputs, so what's live is identical to what was audited.

Who it's for, and why it matters

An audit or procurement-oversight team can't investigate 12.1 million contracts by hand. This turns that haystack into a ranked, explainable shortlist: which suppliers and buyers to look at first, with the reasoning attached to every flag, so a non-technical investigator can defend the decision to act. The same instinct, spend that regulators can question and you can answer, is what I brought to financial-data validation at Deloitte.
